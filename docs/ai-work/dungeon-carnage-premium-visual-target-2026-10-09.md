# C64 Dungeon Carnage — approved premium visual standard
**Decision:** 9 October 2026 | **Status:** approved product target, staged implementation, not yet visually shipped
**Architecture:** existing JavaScript Canvas renderer, canonical gameplay owners and shared browser/itch.io/Windows source. No Unity/Godot/Phaser replacement.

## The owner's visual reference
Owner supplied a top-down pixel-art dungeon combat scene with dark stone corridors and voids, large brick wall faces, wall flames producing orange lighting, moss/worn stone, visually grouped crates/barrels/debris, readable player and enemies, impressive projectile/explosion effects, and a compact resource HUD. Match the **level of detail, lighting, atmosphere, consistent scale and game feel**; DO NOT replicate the screenshot's copyrighted sprites/tiles/maps, text, art, branding or HUD. It is a style-quality reference, not a repository art asset or a commercial licence.

Target style: a cohesive atmospheric CCG pixel dungeon with recognisable CCG hero, 15 differentiated themes, dramatic but readable warm-versus-cool local lighting, strong foreground silhouettes, restrained environmental detail and effective in-game feedback, without compromising exploration, traps, multiplayer-retirement guards, game progression, save data or mobile performance.

## Existing source — do not duplicate
- `arcade/lost-sizzler/js/game-render.js` already owns `drawTile`, `drawTilePerformance`, `drawWallLights`, `drawDynamicLighting`, `drawFog`, `drawFurniture`, `drawEffects`, `drawAmbientMotes`, `drawEnemy`, `drawPlayer`, `renderView`, adaptive quality selection and scene/camera geometry.
- Approved authored sprites, wall/floor/door/chest/trap frames, torch-sconce/fireplace frames and fallback renderers already exist. `js/asset-overrides.js` and `assets/asset-manifest.json` catalogue them. Existing R118 assets and `docs/ai-work/dungeon-carnage-visual-overhaul.md` record licensing; preserve all rejections (including non-commercial-only/unknown-rights packs).
- World, wall/door collision, floor objectives, sanctuary protection, attack and trap timing are the game engine's responsibility. Drawing cannot determine gameplay state or physical passability. Avoid parallel lighting clocks or new gameplay owners.

## Locked visual requirements
1. **Architecture / floors / wall depth:** improve weathered stone, mortar/corners, wall fronts/tops, floor variation and deep background space. Keep authored biomes distinct, important walkways/doors/secret walls and hazard warnings visible; don't flatten everything into one dark palette. Retain pixel density and coherent geometry.
2. **Warm torchlight against cool shadow:** richer state-driven wall and held torches, flickering flame frames, restrained radial illumination and ambience. Use approved four-frame sconces when their orientation and native scale work; keep existing fallback and fog-of-war. Never hide enemy/hazard/player silhouettes under darkness or reduce needed accessibility contrast.
3. **Contextual room dressing:** deterministic purpose-aware clusters of barrels, crates, banners, cobwebs, rubble, moss, foliage, bone piles and furnishings selected from provenanced existing art. Only place AFTER reserved geometry and critical objects; enforce safe path/door/exit/trader/sanctuary/healing tile/key/quest/puzzle/boss placement, collision-to-art agreement and tile-scale suitability. Decoration may not create softlocks.
4. **Animation continuity:** unified engine-state selection for player/enemy idle/move/attack/hit/death; flashing torches, fireplaces, traps, chests and switch states. Approved CCG hero/named enemy identities stay recognisable. Animation time and frames never drive damage, cooldowns, movement or collision.
5. **Combat feel:** responsive firearm muzzle flash, FIRE/SHOCK/SPIKE readability, restrained sparks, impact, smoke and optional splatter that cannot obscure enemies, loot, trap warnings or control inputs. Stay within existing effect pool limits and frame-rate quality tiers; support reduced-motion settings. No excessive shake/strobes.
6. **Camera and HUD:** large usable game field with consistent pan/zoom; compact, thematic and readable health, ammo, objectives, keys and minimap on wide-screen and mobile portrait/landscape. Don't copy the reference HUD; preserve established input/fullscreen/minimap/player tracking.
7. **Package consistency:** browser and Windows/itch.io must render the same authored presentation and gameplay. Avoid remote art dependencies, duplicated assets, asset-file exposure promises or unapproved paid package publishing.

## Acceptance for EVERY visual code change
- Source-owner inspection, real baseline and image/animation comparison at native in-game pixel scale BEFORE claiming it looks better. At least corridor, sanctuary healing tile, shop, challenge/puzzle, boss and two different biomes.
- No new sprite from arbitrary source. Retain exact creator/pack/license/source/local file/import/modifications record; no extra packs unless justified.
- Preserve simulation/attack, objective gates, walkability, saves, progression, enemy identity, player controls, fog, minimap and original sound owners. Test headless Node plus applicable 6-shard Chromium and package-specific runs.
- Test rich/reduced/severe renderer, actual FPS/frame time, GPU/CPU/memory and mobile orientation. Set performance budgets from measured baseline; no made-up percentage improvements.
- Keep a fallback at every optional asset/animation decode boundary. Maintain reduced-motion effects and imageSmoothing disablement.
- For every slice record **implemented / automated green / packaging verified / deployed / owner gameplay accepted** separately, with exact GitHub PR/head, before-after captures and performance numbers. Never claim completed from documentation alone.
