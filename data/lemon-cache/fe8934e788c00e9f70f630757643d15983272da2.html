Neo-Rio 2021-09-06
Fantastic head-to-head maze shooting game. This is one of those rare arcade conversions that bests the fun and playability of the source material. The arcade version had a shamefully steep difficulty curve - designed to kick you off and take your money after your mandatory two minutes of playtime. The C64 conversion doesn't have enemies hitting warp speed on you, so the game remains balanced and fair, and with some strategy you can get good at the game. Throw in a magic voice cartridge and you'll even get the arcade game speech!
It is definitely worth a play - either solo or with a friend. You could play competitively or co-op, as long as you didn't shoot each other. We would not have so much co-op fun until Atari's Gauntlet came along.

hazzard 2017-04-25 (10/10)
This game is a very rare example of atmosphere and imagination trumping graphics. When you see screenshots of WoW it looks bland and utterly forgettable, but it plays brilliantly and has a surreal ambiance to it which draws you in and never lets up.
The simple 4-note melody between levels sends shivers down your spine and the in-game music makes your hair stand on end!
When the Wizard himself appears and teleports like a madman around the screen, there's no better feeling than drilling him with your gun and seeing the maze flash like crazy! Playing with two players is even better - lots of fun to be had.
If you never experienced this game as a kid, PLAY IT NOW!

Disc Master I 2004-10-01 (10/10)
Recently, a friend & I hosted a Commodore 8-bit and Amiga exhibit at a North East USA Classic Gaming Expo. This was our first time and we didn't really know if the Commodore line would be appreciated by common gaming fans. Throughout the day many people would stop by and play a game or two on either the Vic-20, C64, or Amiga systems. My friend & I were happy that we at least had some visitors to our tables. Then I popped in Wizard of Wor and that infamous music started playing. This is when things started getting crazy. Visitors young and old (pre teens - grandparents) started converging on the area. I overheard some comments saying "Hey, where is that music coming from? I know that game! " or "LOOK! Thats Wizard of Wor! Lets play! ". In a flash our booth was getting crowded and the visitors loved playing WoW. I kept WoW on for over 2 or 3 hours straight and it wasn't until the final 15 min that the area settled down. I still remember a few times a group of friends would be playing and then they'd start yelling and at one point I thought a fight was going to happen because one guy shot the other and ended his game. It was definitely a fun experience and the fact that WoW was so well recognized and appreciated by people of all ages is a true testament to the greatness of that timeless classic. Hail Wizard of Wor!
