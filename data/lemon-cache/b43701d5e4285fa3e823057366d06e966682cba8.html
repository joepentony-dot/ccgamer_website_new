Deist 2025-04-19 (9/10)
In case this wasn't mentioned before, Rodland on Amiga is not only a great conversion but quite simply the ultimate version of the game: that's right people, it surpasses the original arcade game! The only example of that I know of on a 16/32-bit machine (together with Pac-Mania, although that one is subject to discussion I guess...).

Production-wise, a good deal from the arcade original graphics is here (including cut-scenes, but missing the 1st fairy picture + possibly some bosses/backgrounds), more "transposed" than "converted" as they look mostly identical.
Soundwise, musics are different for the most part (except for Extra Time + fairy Extra Life sequences which both versions share, admittedly sounding fuller on arcade, yet slightly tweaked for the better on Amiga), and cuter (and therefore more fitting) on the Amiga. Controls and gameplay too are _mostly_ identical (more on that below).

What then, you may ask, makes this the "ultimate" Rodland version? I refer you to the Wiki page of Rodland for full details, but in a nutshell:
- Additional content: levels+hidden features+bonuses+enemy animations, all absent from the arcade version
- Several control glitches of the original are corrected (enemies stuck on top of ladders, inability to slam enemies down while in air or zap things from ladders) rendering the gameplay on Amiga much more enjoyable!

Haven't tried Rodland yet? Go play it today, the way it was intended to be played... Courtesy of the Amiga!!!

MartinJSUK 2023-01-12 (9/10)
Japanese design expertise and quirkiness married to great Amiga coding. The method of attacking the enemies is one of the funniest sights in an Amiga game, the multiple uses of ladders making for classic seat-of-the-pants strategy that the best action games offer - do you kill enemies now for safety, or dash to get the flowers first and leave enemies for the bonus game and potential extra lives? Levels are suitably varied, the balloons that the enemies also use are a favourite touch of mine. Graphics and sound fit perfectly, controls are responsive and efficient, collision detection just right, and the simultaneous 2P mode survives. With only 40 single-screen levels it's perhaps of below-average difficulty, but otherwise hard to fault.
