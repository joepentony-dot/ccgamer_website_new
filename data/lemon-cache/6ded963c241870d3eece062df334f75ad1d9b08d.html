Colonel Kurtz 2018-04-09 (9/10)
Dune... It captivated me ever since I saw that Virgin logo changing into the Cryo one. From the excellent artistic direction to the effort towards some pretty original gameplay (yet with its fair share of compromise I should say), it had most of the distinctive traits of early Cryo productions - that is, before they also ventured into publishing. Back then even a smooth day/night cycle or a short animation were stuff to dream of. Or should I mention the great tunes, some even outdo the Spice Opera album versions... Too bad there were only three here - Ecolove, Free Men and Sign of the Worm - and the absence of "Chani's Eyes" is unpardonable

It does take some inspiration from the David Lynch film, from Kyle McLachlan's likeness to a few narrative liberties (read 'weirding modules'); and much like the movie, it's an imperfect game that I can't stop loving. They managed to draw quite a thin line between adventure and strategy that somehow works.

So what keeps Dune away from the perfect score? Well, in the end it's more linear than it wants you to believe it is. The game constantly sends signals to the player hinting at the right order to do things, and if you follow that order and heed the advice of every NPC when they give it to you, you'll eventually find out you've beaten the game with little effort. This policy was later taken to an extreme in Lost Eden, for instance. Still it didn't keep me from playing it through several times over.

Shunichiro 2009-04-07 (10/10)
As much as Dune is a compelling adventure/strategy hybrid, it's also an example of a game as an artform. Ethereal atmosphere with stylized visuals and spaceous music draws you deep into the planet Dune to battle rival Harkonnens over the precious spice. This kind of immersion is rare even for today. Modern platforms with their photorealistic graphics and other reality-mimicing assets can't succeed in what a hand-crafted French game did back in 1992.

That's not to say the only strength of the game is in its audiovisual quality. The adventure segment carrying the intriguing story is well-crafted and user-friendly and while the strategy part could have used some fine tuning in playability, it gets the job done. The games literally drives you on to explore its secrets and you aren't satisfied until you have experienced more and more of it.

Dune is Cryo at its very best and how I want to remember them.
