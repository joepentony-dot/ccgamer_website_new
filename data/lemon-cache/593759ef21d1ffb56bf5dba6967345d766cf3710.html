MartinJSUK 2025-03-26 (8/10)
The violent ball-carrying walled-arena Rollerball style action we expect from future-sport games was codified by Speedball. The sequel took 2 years, just in time for a wave of second-rate clones, but worth the wait - almost every feature is expanded or built upon.

The setting is darker with a hint of dystopia, and audiovisuals even more metallic. Coins and temporary bonuses (improving your team or weakening theirs) pop up randomly during the short frantic matches, on a bigger arena than before with stars and multipliers - you can win in different ways, and there's always something happening. The 15 CPU teams vary greatly in playing style and strengths - each of their 9 players have distinct skills.

Better still, the upgrade and transfer system between games adds layers of subtlety to the brutality, typical of the Bitmaps' way of ensuring strategic elements and multiple philosophies to suit different playing styles. You can develop in many ways - either focus on a certain skill across the team, or a specific position (maybe to combat your next opponent?), or splurge on a few key players? It's clever that you can't upgrade too unevenly, so coins must be spent on all areas (including subs) before you can max anything out.

Perfect? No. Like many Bitmaps games there's occasional slowdown, and a truer league structure would've extended life and made ultimate victory less random. Still amazing though, especially two-player. Why oh why did I never buy or really play it BITD?
