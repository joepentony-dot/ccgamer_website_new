Monk 2014-11-03 (9/10)
Cawley, the Atari-version is almost as good as the C64 version. It has a nicer color system (more colors obviously give a nicer variety and spread), but its sound effects are not as unique or interesting - just typical Atari-stuff.

Atari sounds are great of course, but the SID just offers more flexibility, and the generic Atari sounds just do not fit THIS particular game as well as the customized SID-sounds, that bring that last little cherry on the top to complete the gameplay by actually immersing you also aurally a bit more (just compare the 'movement sounds' of Mitch and Flip, how the sounds almost describe the movements, helping with the illusion of 'being there'). The Atari falls a bit short in this department.

The jingles are slightly different, but it's a matter of taste, which you prefer (for me, it's C64 all the way, aurally speaking).

The gameplay is otherwise pretty much identical, though - and it doesn't really matter, which version you choose - at least, with Atari, you get more colors, though you loose the aural-immersion a bit.

Btw, I never thought this game as 'colour the block', I thought it more as 'activate the light'-type. It's more interesting that way - that they are activating some important light circuits instead of just boringly coloring something.

About the ending - perhaps it just repeats the largest levels forever, after you reach level 10 or 11. It's a bit disappointing, if Flip and Mitch can never escape.

Monk 2008-07-17 (9/10)
The controls are very easy and intuitive, once you understand that it is an isometric viewpoint. Down is not down from your own viewpoint, but from Flip's or Mitch's viewpoint. My mom also had trouble with the controls, but I really can't understand it, as it's really very easy if you just have correct orientation of the directions in your head (-8 Of course once you get the hang of it with Flip, then the screen changes upside down and now you guide Mitch, who is actually hanging instead of jumping up and down.. So you have to do a re-calibration of your head.. But after this it really gets intense, and the playability is very immersive. It's only after perhaps 10 levels that you 'wake up' back to the 'normal world' from the intense hypnotism of the game, because of the repetition. I also love how the colors change and have such nice fit together. Ah, those pink screens.. This is really a wonderful game, it doesn't get much better than this.

Monk 2008-07-17 (9/10)
I just played this game with my C64. I don't think it's fair to compare this to Q*Bert, as this is more like it's own genre (which no one really cloned either). This game is from the most energetic era of game development, so the feeling of playing this is wonderful. The gameplay is also great, and although I might have some nostalgia value (as this was one of the first games that made a huge impact on me in my childhood) added to it, I must say this would be a perfect game to play otherwise, except that it repeats itself too much. The levels just get larger and larger, and after 11 levels, I get a bit bored. But it's always fun to trick the zookeeper and his net in many different ways, and the musics are very cute and sounds are appropriate - for example the net makes very menacing sound that fits it perfectly and scares the player. GREAT classic game, without which my childhood wouldn't have been as happy!
