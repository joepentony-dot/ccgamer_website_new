MartinJSUK 2022-11-04 (7/10)
One of the true landmark Amiga games, as pretty much the first game designed for a 1 Megabyte Amiga from the ground up. With its 32-colour 50FPS display, smooth multidirectional scrolling, bold use of sampled ambient sound and speech, careful disguising of loading time, a co-operative 2 player mode, and the atmospheric ingame computer system, finally the potential of a 1 Megabyte Amiga was being fully exploited. Few games generate as brooding and foreboding an atmosphere as this. Almost at a stroke, nobody tried to give us ST ports anymore.

Sadly, the gameplay doesn't live up to this potential. Combining Alien (the film) with Gauntlet (the game) is an awesome idea, but if Ripley had this many respawning aliens attacking her from every direction (with no way of destroying the spawning points), limited ammo and easy-to-waste keys, plus one-way doors which the aliens can inexplicably walk through unaffected, it would have been a very short film. Something as simple as being able to shoot while backtracking would have really boosted the gameplay.

Interpret it as Survival Horror and you can explain some of these issues, and its one of the better co-operative 2-player games too. Still just about worth the effort, but well short of what it should have been. Sadly, technically stunning games made unfairly difficult by design flaws became Team 17's trademark.

Maff Rignall 2005-04-13 (3/10)
Yet another turgid Team 17 game which (like almost every other Team 17 game) simply took another game (Gauntlet), stripped it of all its endearing qualities, dressed it up in 50Hz overscan scrolling and sold it to the Amiga community as revolutionary. And, like almost every other Team 17 game, people bought it. To be fair, the graphics and sound were nice, but the game itself was horrible to play.

And the miserly game design was just painful. Things like enemies spawning on top of you, making you lose energy through no fault of your own. Or how you had to go all the way back to the start if you were late for the elevator (even if you still have heaps of lives left - what's the point of having a life counter if it isn't used?) and the way you could get trapped if you accidentally shot a fire door.

The fire doors were actually a good idea, and all sorts of great things could have been done with them - you could have been forced to find an alternative route, or to locate an override switch to force them open. Or even better, the levels could have been designed so you used the fire door to trap the aliens and confine them to certain parts of the level. But no - no intelligent game design here, the fire doors are simply an excuse to force the player to restart the whole game should they accidentally hit the fire button once. Oh joy.

But hey, it's got a nice intro. And that's what really counts - right?

3/10.
