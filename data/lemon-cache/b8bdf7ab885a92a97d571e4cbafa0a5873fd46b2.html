Colonel Kurtz 2017-11-02 (8/10)
One of the few exceptions I make for platformers, Superfrog is excellent in nearly every respect - technically superb, smooth, easy to control, with secret areas, nods to previous games and what have you.

What is not so great about it? Well, perhaps a certain lack of personality and charisma. It's possibly the "best run-of-the-mill" platform game to ever appear on the Amiga, with yet another generic anthropomorphic animal in an even more generic environment. Sure, the animated intro is fantastic and hilarious (product placement aside, but I guess that can be forgiven), but in the end it's a parody of a fairytale, with not enough to define the game and its characters. With such a fine game behind them, they would have deserved to become iconic, unique.

For the record, I don't think Mario or Sonic are the best examples of that: they're only iconic because their publishers were also the respective console manufacturers and had the firepower to push them into the market. Look at what Earthworm Jim managed to do with an anthropomorphic worm and a damsel in distress: no comparison, it could very well be the most successful attempt in that department... And on the Amiga? Well that's what Zool tried to achieve and failed, and where perhaps Chuck Rock came the closest.

Prob Lem 2013-04-17 (7/10)
Superfrog has a fun intro, cute character-design, nice-enough graphics, and good music, but it's marred by the title character's VERY strange momentum (or complete lack of it, more accurately). He moves at full-speed the second you move the joystick - and, of course, the game uses the dreaded Up-to-jump tradition, making pulling off jumps while trying to deal with other movements much more difficult than it needs to be (in-air movements feel pretty strange and too fast, as well, which can lead to issues). The jumping issue, at least, is improved greatly by playing in an emulator and mapping Up to a button, instead.

Superfrog's sidekick/weapon, Spud, is useless, incidentally - it's near-enough impossible to actually hit enemies with him!

Also, for some utterly bizarre reason, the game was sponsored by the energy drink, Lucozade. I'm probably not the only person who found it odd, and I also suspect that I'm not the only person who never really associated a sports drink with princes transformed into superheroic frogs! (Indeed, in a parodical remake of the intro, Eric W. Schwartz lampooned the product-placement - randelshofer.ch/animations/anims/eric_w_schwartz/Superbfrog3.anim.html . )

Anyway, it's a solid enough game, though not one I find to be as spectacular as some seem to. It isn't of horrendous quality, though, so if you like platformers and can persevere with the odd momentum of Superfrog himself, you may very well like this.

This game's popularity mystifies me. Sure, the music is catchy and the graphics are smoothly-shaded (albeit lacking in character and devoid of technical effects - they didn't even bother with a copper gradient in the background!), but the level designs are unfair and the controls are PAINFUL. The spacious opening levels are tantalizingly fun to explore, but any hope for lasting appeal is obliterated by the later worlds, which are cramped and loaded with cheaply-placed hazards (including the ever-present instant-death spikes, which somehow manage to kill Superfrog even when he approaches from the side), blind spots, and leaps of faith. This would be annoying even if the controls had been executed with a shred of competence, but Superfrog simply runs too fast, jumps too high, and is generally a mess to move, especially since he's wigglier than Bubsy the Bobcat when airborne. Superfrog could (and should) have been a great game, but these ridiculous design flaws cripple it completely.
