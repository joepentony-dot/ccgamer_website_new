MartinJSUK 2025-02-22 (7/10)
Andy Hutchinson's light-hearted though barbed manual (what did the Welsh do to him?) sets the tone for the Amiga's playful subversives to leave the scene by taking on a sport that may seem staid and overly-formal to outsiders. Not aimed at fans of PGA and Nick Faldo and the like - with no wind, stance adjustments or Greens In Regulation stats this may have appeared bare-bones.

It's joystick controlled, with presentation and features clearly aimed for multiplayer fun. CPU players are nicknamed and are cutely drawn (white and black, pleasingly). Neat ambient sound, while text and speech gently rib poor performance ('still [player]' if you need successive shots, 'Monster Greeny' or the "at last!" speech if you really struggle).

You'll get used to those quickly, as it's a tough game to learn despite conceptual simplicity (and 4 skill levels). It's much easier to miss the second power-bar press and thus not hit the ball than in rival games, but it's the excessively hilly greens (too unrealistic in this case) that cost lots of strokes- at least the holes often accept overhit shots.

The plethora of courses are fictional, given location settings which have no bearing on the bunkers, fairways, ball lies and water hazards you face. Holes are are reused or slightly varied across the courses, but there's still plenty. The deliberate unrealism of some layouts makes the integrated map, on which the shot direction can be set, a great feature.

Not their best, but there's fun to be had.

Madfly 2011-01-09 (8/10)
Well Graham I can relate to your comments on the nightmare to develop. I converted it from the Amiga to the PC. I remember thinking it nearly finished and then finally getting an update from Sensible - to see it had been completely redesigned.
One amusing story to share... Virgin asked at very short notice - for a demo that could fit on a floppy containing a few holes of one of the courses. This meant an all nighter to get it ready - to my relief I got it done and mastered on a floppy in time for the courier, so it could get to the publishers. To my horrior I'd left my test sound effects in it - so there were 450,000 floppies given away free with their coke drinks in and around the London area - when playing, if the ball hit a tree, you would hear my voice saying the word 'TREE'. WE don't need to mention what I said when the ball went down the hole ... Sigh! If I remember rightly I think I did get a call from Pete and it didn't go too well!! :-)

As games go - it's the multiplayer side that made this a heap better. Still a good game thou for single player.

One of Sensible's weaker games. It was a nightmare to develop, being constantly delayed and rewritten, and though the end result didn't justify the wait, it's a simple fun game with some mates - it's easy to get into and it is, of course, instantly recognisable as a Sensible game. As a single-player game, it's good for a while but a lack of variety and atmosphere, as well as the extraordinarily difficult putting, means it isn't as good as it should have been.
