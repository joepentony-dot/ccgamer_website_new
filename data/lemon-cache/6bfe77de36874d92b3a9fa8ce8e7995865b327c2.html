Monk 2014-09-27
Interesting, that the VIC-20 game "Skyhawk" is faster in tempo, allows multiple missiles to be shot in a very rapid fashion, and has multiple enemies onscreen at once - Falcon Patrol only has two at any given time. How limited is that?

And WHY is it so limited, when a 'lesser' computer can have more?

Of course the scrolling is jerky in Skyhawk, and the sounds are... Humm.. 'different'. But it looks like a more exciting game.

However, Falcon Patrol is a classic in many ways, and for many reasons. I never bumped into this game as a kid - I only had an encounter with Falcon Patrol II, which I liked a lot.

But I found this game lately, and realized that it has a great atmosphere, and incredibly well-used noise+filter-sound that is hard to implement during the modern times.

I have been dabbling with some BASIC languages, and it'd be almost impossible to duplicate this kind of a sound, unless it becomes possible to use HardSID-library within the BASIC.

Monk 2014-09-27
It's sad that we lost the "realtime-synth-personality-ability" when we got the "gillion-bits and killion Hz-rate samples"-ability.

I used to think that the FM synth cards for the DOS-PCs sounded pretty good and interesting in many ways, but of course the SID still sounded better.

Instead of us getting a "Super-SID" (meaning, as personal, as atmospheric, as inspiring - but a more advanced version of it - customizable waveforms, for example, and 'unlimited' channels, or at least 8 or more, each with its own filter - but the filters could still be combined to produce the 'analog distortion', and all kinds of combining of different channels could be possible), we just got boring samples.

To reproduce this sound, an awful lot of samples would have to be used, and an incredibly clever program would have to be written, instead of just directly manipulating the filter in the program. That's something I miss a lot.

But besides the atmosphere and the sound, there's not much to this game. And red REALLY clashes with blue!
