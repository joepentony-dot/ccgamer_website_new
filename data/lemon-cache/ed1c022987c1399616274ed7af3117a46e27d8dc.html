bronstein 2016-08-02 (9/10)
@Pyramidenkopf: I think it has to do with the old rivalry between Britons and Germans. Arcade conversions for 8bit systems were the domain of British software houses, and a game programmed in Continental Europe - although released by a British company - was intuitively perceived as a threat to a successful domestic industry. I'm not saying that the British reviewers were bribed (at least not in this case), but I'm pretty confident that when comparing the average ratings of German and British games in the respective mags, in both you will find a tendency of heavier criticism towards foreign games (i. E. On average higher ratings for domestic games).

Pyramidenkopf 2014-12-12 (8/10)
The C64 conversion of R-Type is one of those games I always feel I have to defend. What are those hilariously low ratings in most UK magazines? Yes, this game should be played from disk, which was a problem in a country where most C64 owners were using tapes, but as a game it's not that bad. Some stuff was lost in translation, because the conversion is basicly R-Type quickly crammed into the Katakis engine, and the sprite multiplexor probably couldn't handle stuff like the rotating ring of enemies in level 1. But no matter how much praise the Spectrum version got (it IS very impressive), I still prefer the C64 version for the great sound, the fitting colors and the smoothness of everything, which makes this version feel more like an arcade game than the more accurate Speccy port. It's not at all perfect and slightly buggy, but this is probably the best conversion we could get back then. The team of the first attempt to port this could have never made a better conversion than the one released. I think reading the story behind the port and looking at the v1 previews makes that pretty clear.

COMMENT: Very interesting story here. Katakis was released in September 1988 by Rainbow Arts, however it never got a release in the UK as Activision had it cancelled because of similarities to R-Type, to which Activision had bought the conversion rights. Activision were getting nowhere with their conversion of R-Type and they liked what they saw of Katakis, so they hired the same programming team (Manfred Trenz and others) to do the C64 conversion. They only got 6 weeks to write the conversion, and sadly, it shows. Katakis was eventually altered and released as Denaris in 1989. Tunes #2 to #14 all cover of the arcade game.
