Microprosefan 2010-10-25
Cinemaware always claimed they wished to create an interactive movie rather than a game.
"Defender of the Crown" is a great experience while still being a neat little game. It will always keep you occupied for 30- 45 minutes at least.

Timing is everything. Use Robin's Aid to quickly gain an advantage and with skill and timing you'll be able to besiege an enemy castle in no time.
Your first army should at least have 1 catapult and a knight or 2. Spend the rest on soldiers. Use allied Saxon territory to get the quickes route to enemy Norman castles.

The siege is a great opportunity to waste enemy defenders, combined with Robin's sneak attack this should ensure you win your first siege.

After that, raid enemy castles when they have gained enough wealth and before they can spend it (you'll quickly learn when that happens).

I used to be quite adept at jousting on my C=64 with my "The Arcade" joystick but on my emulator with just the keyboard I suck at it. No matter, just joust for honour and even if you lose you will still trash your enemies on the battlefield if you play wisely.

The worst thing that can happen to you (the player) during a session is: No Saxon Maiden In Distress! Without a beatiful Saxon wife, you simply don't feel as victorious after your final winning battle.

Try to have a knights-only army for the final battle and wipe those Norman scum off the field!

gagnedd 2008-09-12 (10/10)
Key strategy points to winning and winning often

- be aggressive right from the start -- take territories to gain vassels before buying your first set of troops - raid castles often, especially after buying troops - buy a decent army and a catapult, and take over a norman lords castle as soon as possible to take all his territory and gain a huge income advantage.

- balance your troops - a Knight is only good if it has 10 soldiers to protect it, but an army without knights won't stand up against an army with knights unless it has an unbelievably large amount of soldiers. I suggest buying 1 knight to every 10 soldiers or so, and eventually add a catapult or two merely for bombarding purposes (you always need one for sieging castles)

- always save the saxon maid - free income and territory, and one less friend who may decide to turn on you if it becomes too convenient

- after the early parts of the game, keep your home castle protected either by keeping a decent army in it or by surrounding it with friendly territory (better)

- take over as much of the map as possible to dominate with imcome, and therefore, army
