Who knew that moneyless resource management could make such an engrossing, absorbing and downright addictive game? With well-laid-out menus, real character in the people you see on screen, and visual representations of every activity, the result is accessible and fairly quick to learn (though the manual isn't perfect), but with subtleties you never quite stop learning, and no two attempts at each level are the same. The two-mice two-player mode is a lovely bonus too. Makes use of extra memory for more sound and bigger level, but a 512+512 system gets plenty to enjoy. Cute yet mature in a manner no other Amiga game can match.

Colonel Kurtz 2011-02-10 (8/10)
All the praise I can read in here holds for me as well. The Settlers is indeed as addictive as it is said to be, and sports a revolutionary economic system that is not currency-based.

It does have its downsides. For one thing, the slow pace could make a single match tedious at times. I bet most people would gladly have spent half as much time glued to the screen, had they only had a fast forward option (maybe on 68030+ hardware). Then the exclusively iconic user interface is sometimes counterintuitive, and even with the manual in your hands, a simple tooltip would have been really helpful. Finally, unlike the DOS version it does not support Hi-res, even on high-end Amigas.

This is not to sound 'against-the-grain' or anything: The Settlers is still a great game without a doubt. Its flaws pale if compared to the overwhelming pros, yet I cannot lie about them being there.
