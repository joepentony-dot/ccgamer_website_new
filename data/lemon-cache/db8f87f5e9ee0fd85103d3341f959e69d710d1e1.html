Monk 2015-02-13
Like always, some commenters are spot-on, some commenters are wrong, some commenters are ignorant and clueless, and some commenters just make erroneous claims.

Pitfall II wasn't "superior" or "better", and the C64 version of Pitfall certainly does NOT have "better graphics" than the Atari 2600 version.

Too many people equate higher resolution or more polygons to automatically mean "better graphics", without realizing what makes graphics good or bad or better or worse.

Monk 2015-02-13
Atari 2600's graphics are better, because it has better color selection, and it has been used better. It is more pleasing to the eye. And the Atari's lores actually looks GOOD, so it's debatable at best, whether destroying that nice lores-look is "better" or "worse". I vote for "worse".

The C64-version also has an annoying dithering-effect on the trees, which creates a huge moiré pattern, which also makes the graphics look odd, weird and worse than the clear and nice, natural-looking Atari 2600-version graphics.

The Atari 2600 version is the best, like in most cases. For some reason, when they converted stuff from Atari 2600 to C64, something important was always lost, and the result was always a pitiful inflated balloon or a shadow of its former glory. Some games fared better than others, for example, River Raid is a GOOD conversion - but the Atari 2600 version is still slightly better.

H. E. R. O. Might be the only conversion that is actually better than the original (although the original is still great).

They even did this with C16-C64-conversions. Fire Ant and Formula One are great examples of how to kill the spirit, excitement and looks of a game when you port it to the C64. Perhaps they were just learning the C64 at the time, but still.

Monk 2015-02-13
Pitfall C64-version is not awful, it's pretty good, and relatively close to the original, but as someone mentioned, the scorpion is almost impossible, making this version worse, plus the colors are just not right - there should be more harmonious green. Though the poor C64 had such a bad selection of green - one that's too bright and looks almost yellow (at least to a slightly colorblind people), and one that's nice, but then, it's very much alone.

But play the Atari 2600 version, and you will hear the excitement, you will see the excitement and harmonious glory of the original graphics, and you will feel the excitement as you experience the fully tweaked, perfect gameplay. The C64 gameplay is slightly off, in a way that's hard to explain, besides the scorpion.

And as implied, the C64 version's sound effects are not as interesting as the Atari version's, although they are pretty simple.

There's also too much BLACK in the C64 version. The ropes are now suddenly black, the pants and hair of Pitfall Harry are suddenly black (and then they awkwardly CHANGE COLOR when he goes down the ladder!), and the rope is also so thin that it's harder to spot properly (which is probably why they changed it to black).

Monk 2015-02-13
It's like, they took a great, perfectly playable game, and worsened it in all aspects a little bit, and that's the C64 version. I thought I hated Pitfall, until I got it in the mail for real Atari 2600, and plugged it in, turned it on, displayed it on a bright, big, CRT TV and played it..

Yes, it's that much different. I went from hate (C64 version) to love (Atari version) to "It's not too bad after all" (C64 version after playing the Atari version).

You could say the Atari 2600 version saved this game from my hatred, by showing that it's actually an excellent, and esoterically exciting game.

By the way, Pitfall II is not superior, it's inferior. It destroyed the immersion of 'wide open spaces in an unlimited forest'-feeling into 'narrow platforms on which you must navigate yourself in a very frustrating way and find where you have to go in completely identical sceneries without a map'.

Pitfall (Atari 2600) is an excellent game, Pitfall (C64) is an O. K. Game, Pitfall II is an atrocity that should be shot.

You are looking at one of the pillars of classic gaming. Supposedly invented only because David Crane had come up with an impressive running-man animation and wanted to design a game to show it off, Pitfall! Is the quintessential platform game. Mario, Sonic, Crash, and Banjo & Kazooie all owe their very lives to the intrepid Pitfall Harry and his groundbreaking adventure. While woefully primitive-seeming today, it is nonetheless as important to history as Pac-Man or Space Invaders. Sadly, none of the ports to other machines were ever quite able to capture the magic of the Atari 2600 original, and the C-64 is no exception...
