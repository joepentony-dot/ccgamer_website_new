RootyKazooty 2018-05-21 (10/10)
I totally agree with Zzap! 's 96% rating. It's so much more fun than the first game. Exploring the gorgeous and varying levels for pickups and trying to find the exit to the next level is epic.
It's very exciting not knowing what monsters are waiting in the next screen (they're randomized) and trying to remember if there are pits to leap over. You can fall straight down if you get it badly wrong, or teeter on the edge where you have to waggle the joystick fast to regain your balance. I love that variety. The enemies have it in their attacks too, giving them a realistic unpredictability.
Barbarian II has hands down the best enemy design of any C64 game. There's a stunning cast of fantasy creatures which are very detailed, have lovely animations, and often laugh and taunt you after they kill you. I particularly like the wolf-like beast that crouches and springs at your head, knocking you down and sometimes eating you to death, and the dragons that can bite your head off and swallow it. You see it going down their neck as your decapitated character crashes to their knees. You can chop some of the enemies heads off too.
Barbarian II is a truly brilliant, creative game which is the best the C64 has to offer.
I rate it as highly as Turrican II, The Last Ninja 1+2, and Creatures 1+2.

Monk 2015-07-15
There are various problems with this game.

First of all, it IS a bit of a cash-cow, made to bring more moolah after the success of the first game, that was made with a passion to create a great game.

Secondly, the fighting system is slow, awkward, and delayed, and absolutely doesn't fit in this type of a game at all. You can come to terms with it, but it's still frustrating and clunky. The C64 can do better.

Third thing that sinks this game is the level design. It's basically a maze, where you have no idea where you have been and where to go, even with the 'compass' system. It's completely illogical - you can go left, and appear on the left side of the next screen. What the.. ?

Fourth example of crappiness this game oozes is the monotonous backgrounds. The graphics-style itself is good enough, nothing to complain about, but the exact same scenery is drawn on every screen of a level. You only get variety when you change levels. Even with this kind of limited idea of "rocky mountain backgrounds", SOME variety could have been introduced, for crying out loud. If you see one picture of each level, you have basically seen all of this game's graphical variations. Why can't there be different settings for different screens? Would have made it feel like EXPLORATION. Now you feel like a mouse in a boring maze, trying to find the way out.

Monk 2015-07-15
There are good sides, like the sound effects, the graphics style, the animations, and the big monsters. But the downsides drown the game.

I guess it seemed like a good idea on paper; take the great fighting game, Barbarian, and instead of just having to fight on one, beautifully drawn screen, you can move around and explore and there are different monsters and such! Yay!

And on SOME level, it sort of works - it's fun to bash the monsters sometimes, and some of them seem human-ish. But no real duels between humans anymore, and that's a downside. It's the same mistake that so many gamemakers made; from Wolfenstein 3D and shooting humans, we go to Doom and shooting some generic monsters. It's just not the same. (Though Doom is an excellent classic of course, and can't be faulted)

Which brings more tension and immersion; having to struggle against other human beings, or having to struggle against.. Some.. Cartoon monsters? That's the final nail in the coffin.

Some monsters are 'realistic' or 'respectable', and cool to fight against, but some are just ridiculous, cartoonish crap that feels like you are fighting a saturday morning kid's show, rather than a realistic, barbaric world, like in "Barbarian". There was a seriousness and realism among the lores artistical beauty. THAT is gone in this game. This one is very cartoonish.

Maff Rignall 2003-09-19
After the great original, this is something of a disappointment. The complex array of fighting moves which was the guts of the gameplay has been all but removed, and an 'arcade adventure' element introduced to take its place. Problem is, the 'arcade adventure' simply involves following a compass to the end of the level and the odd locked door. Hardly engaging stuff - and as mentioned earlier, sometimes a pit is blocked by a monster, forcing you to give up one of your lives in order to get past it. Where this game shines however is in the monsters - imaginatively designed, beautifully drawn, wonderfully animated. They all have different ways of killing your character too, which means the main attraction of this game is finding new ways to die so you can ogle the death sequences. It's entertaining for a while, but the lack of real substance makes it hard for me to see this game as anything other than a short lived novelty act. 6/10.
