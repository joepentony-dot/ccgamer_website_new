Colonel Kurtz 2021-02-28 (7/10)
I played the demo back in the day, but left the full game on the shelf for 30 years, and that's what killed it for me. Looking at it today it's still a solid game, but the flaws in both the action and puzzle-solving elements are hard to ignore.

The action is marred by the ambiguous controls (no 2-button standard → lots of accidental switching to the inventory instead of jumping), awkward weapon throwing (there would have been nothing wrong with a little auto-aiming), and the fact that Karadoc doesn't cast a shadow.

Puzzles are not always as logical as they're claimed to be: I've come across quite a few instances of levers you mustn't pull, objects you mustn't pick up with no hint to it, and undocumented cause-effect relationships. It's pretty easy to end up in a walking dead situation and not know it, if you so much as have used a spell/potion anywhere but where absolutely necessary.

It's not bad design in itself: more like a gameplay style that's deeply rooted in its predecessors from the 1980's, and to which the modern-day gamer is just no longer used. However, it just doesn't sit well with me to give it the 8/10 that the demo compelled me to.

mike_tyson 2012-05-17
Cadaver is action-adventure in isometric perpspective, done in a style similar to 8-bit classics such as Knight Lore or Pentagram by 'ULTIMATE Play The Game'. This genre was super-successful on 8-bit home computers, and there were many, many fantastic games by ULTIMATE and other companies done in that style. On the Amiga, there are only very few games in this genre, Cadaver being one of the best. You just won't find the magic of the earlier ULTIMATE games here: the graphics are good and done in typical Bitmap Brothers - style, the sound sucks, the gameplay is very oldschool and the interface feels unresponsive. A decent game, but not really worth revisiting. If you like this genre I really recommend checking out the old ULTIMATE classics on the 8-bit platform of your choice instead.

mondoshawan 2012-02-26 (5/10)
Interesting game, but I was let down by the troublesome controls. Fire both jumps, throws/attacks, and pops you into a menu that pauses the game when near items. Spacebar pops up a menu for the last item grabbed or used in the enter menu. Enter brings up your inventory.

Sound-wise it reminded me more of an Atari 2600/5200 rather than an Amiga -- congratulations sounds were very monotimbral and monophonic and there is no music. The visuals are great for an OCS game, but like so many isometric games on the Amiga, there's no shadows for objects in the air, which means it's difficult to tell where those objects are in relation to anything else. Fortunately, the positioning of objects isn't too difficult to work out, but throwing things I found to be rather difficult at best, which means attacking and manipulating things was fiddly.
