hazzard 2017-04-09 (3/10)
A woeful conversion - Zzap!64 must've been bribed to award this atrocious game 86%.
I will give some credit to the backdrops which are recognizable from the arcade, however the rest of the game is severely lacking. The sprites are far too blocky and just don't look right. In regards to the gameplay, the enemies attack you with relentless zeal using bullets you can barely see, let alone avoid!
You can toggle between music and SFX, however there's only one tune for the entire game which quickly grates on your soul.
Then there's the issue with crouching - the arcade version let you crouch to avoid bullets, however for the C64, crouching is achieved by pushing SHIFT, however the game will make you stand up again at arbitrary moments, usually when you're in the middle of a firefight so you'll get blasted to smithereens. Avoid.😒

Takumaji 2011-08-21 (5/10)
I was looking forward to Narc C64 from the day I read the Zzap! Review, sadly the game turned out quite disappointing. It's neither an accurate converson, nor too exciting in the gameplay department. Graphics-wise, the game offers smooth scrolling, the sprites look very blocky though, levels/backdrops are okay but could have been better. Sound and f/x are above average to good. Gameplay is solid but there's little that stands out, except for the extra weapons and two-player co-op. I guess the high number of concurrent sprites on screen is one of the reasons why the dev'ers made them less detailed in order to fit them into the 64's RAM. All in all, Narc C64 is a decent sidescrolling shooter with a high difficulty level and rather mindless non-stop blasting action that could have been much more with some extra polish and a more balanced difficulty. It is still beyond me how the game managed to score 86% in the Zzap! Review, 60+% would have been more approriate in my opinion. 5/10.

superpacman 2007-05-17 (3/10)
The arcade game was designed by Eugene Jarvis who was responsible for Defender and Robotron 2084. It was released on the first 32 bit arcade hardware. As such its pedigree was of a high calibre.

Translating this to an 8-bit machine was always going to be something of a compromise, but the gameplay of this version pales into comparison to the original.

Graphically and sonically I think they did the best they could, but ultimately key aspects of the gameplay failed to be transferred.
