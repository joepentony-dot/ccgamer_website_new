Monk 2015-02-28
This does not work with Commodore 128. What a disappointment, would've been a perfect game to play while making food (my C128 is right next to the nourishment fabrication facilities).

Argh!

The game itself has the same kind of legendary gameplay as International Soccer, or "Commodore Soccer", as some people have been someitmes known to lovingly call it.

Why can't they make games like this anymore? Just pure gameplay, where even the tiniest things (like audience clapping) feel impressive.

Nowadays it's 90% just WATCHING a game, and 10% interrupted gameplay (well, that might be a slight exaggeration, but try to play some of the DS/N64/Wii games for comparison, where there is tons of unskippable, trivial, boring, uninspired intros, cutscenes and such. "Nights into Dreams" was a great classic for Sega Saturn (and the PS2 version is pretty good, too), but the Wii-version forces you to watch so much crap that when you finally get to play two seconds before having to watch another cutscene, you are ready to go Berzerk).

Sorry for digressing, but then again, how much can you talk about a basketball game?

IPAndrews 2004-06-16
I like basketball as a game sport, and I think this parrticular implementation plays better than many modern ones. The passing is accurate and interception is predictable. What I mean is if you make a pass with an opposing player in the way, the pass WILL be intercepted. Unlike say NBA Jam where the pass will often go through anyway. This means that
a) you know where you stand
b) you have to play a slower more careful passing game, which I prefer.
Gray1971 2004-05-03
I have never enjoyed basketball as a spectator sport, and latter-day sims such as NBA Jam leave me cold. But this game is a truly timeless classic that can rise above my apathy for the sport. Although its range of moves is fairly simple, they offer the player a great sense of fludity and freedom in choosing when to pass and where to run. I think that this sense of control and fluidity has been lost from the more recent sports sims by the need to use ten different joystick buttons for a vast range of so-called special moves.
