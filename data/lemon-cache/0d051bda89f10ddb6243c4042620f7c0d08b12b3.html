liam_sohal 2023-05-05 (10/10)
This was my main Xmas present back in 1992 (£35, which was a bit pricey at the time, and probably equivalent to £70 in today's prices!). Like many of us, it's been one of my favourite games throughout the decades (except the lousy 'Call to Power' version). It can however be a dangerous waste of time, and it's all too easy to waste a whole Sunday on it rather than doing something more sensible outdoors. I have to say this is one of those Amiga games that it doesn't really make sense to play today - modern PCs can do it much better (whereas I'll quite happily spend a few hours playing some of the platformers and arcade games on my A600). Mind you, I have to confess I do find myself looking at the Amiga screenshots and envying the relative simplicity of the game back then - even though I love playing the recent versions they've become so complicated and detailed in terms of technologies, civics, diplomacy etc that the casual gamer who just dips in every so often will find themselves making lots of fairly random decisions. Whereas with the original, I did actually grasp pretty much most of the rules and mechanics of the game. Even the scan of the box brings back memories, it was unusually high class packaging and the gold really set it off. I do notice now that the box says “hard disk recommended”, something I must have merrily overlooked at the time. The disk swapping wasn’t that bad on this one.

MartinJSUK 2022-12-23 (10/10)
Sid Meier's strategic masterpiece was wonderfully converted to the Amiga, with everything surviving intact. A perfect balance of exploration, military planning, research and diplomacy, with the turn-based structure allowing for maximum planning ahead. Layers of gameplay, with surprises and challenges at every turn. It looks daunting at first, I liked the look of it when it was new but put off buying it until I was a bit older and wiser, and I can't say I grasped it all at first, but persistence and absorbing the big manual yielded amazing rewards. Turn off the animations and pointless Palace screens to limit swaps on a one-drive system. One of the last games I played regularly before the necessary-evil move to a PC - and Civ 2 was an early buy once I did.

steef-online 2004-12-22 (10/10)
This must be one of the best games ever made! Why? First of all the immense size of this game. There is so much in it! You'll start at the stoneage with one unit and you’ll advance through time. Build cities and extract food and building materials from the surrounding land. It's very critical where you place your cities because every peace of land has it's own advantages and disadvantages. You have to feed your people and you can build units. Units of war, diplomacy, trade, construction etc. The units you can build will become more advanced the more advanced your society is. So you have to invent scientifical breakthroughs too. An extra feature of this game which is brilliant is that every city is a world of its own, which needs to be built up. Every building in your city has a function. Every city has a certain population with their own needs. So you really have to put effort in keeping everybody happy or else they will revolt and when things really get messy they even revert to the enemy! There are also other populations with whom you can trade, go to war with or work together with, and each has its own features. Some are violent and others are good traders, etc. The sheer number of units you can built is enormous! And with so much in this game, everything is so perfectly balanced! If you are a fan of strategy games and you have never played this, be ashamed! Get it and play it!
