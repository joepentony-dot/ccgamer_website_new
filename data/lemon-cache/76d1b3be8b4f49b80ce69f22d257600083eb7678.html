mike_tyson 2012-07-04
Graphics: Agony has some of the best graphics on the original Amiga hardware: lush, detailed, with very good color choices. The animated backgrounds and enemies are surprisingly original and varied, and done in a Roger Dean-esque style typical of Psygnosis. Minimalistic (read: nonexistent) interface: scores and remaining lives are not displayed. Fine with me.

Sound: Psygnosis really broke the mold here. 8 musicians, 2 graphics artists, and 1 programmer! You would expect it backwards. The result is outstanding music and nondescript sound effects. The soundtrack is mostly instrumental/symphonic, no metal or pure synth sounds as in many other games of this genre.

Gameplay: As a shooting game, Agony is average. It's certainly no Death Smiles or Cotton 2, but it ain't shitty either. The hit box of the owl is certainly too big. There are many enemies on-screen at the same time, but only few bullets. Action is fast. I really like it that they did not commit the worst sin of game design and make this a checkpoint shooter: in Agony, if you loose a live there is no interrruption, you just carry on. This is the way it should be.

The loading times are so long that each level starts in pause mode by default! Not a bad idea if you think about it.

Agony is overall a very good game and a typical example of a game that in this form has only been done on the Amiga. Graphics and music are top-notch, the gameplay is fun.

A nice fun fact about the title music: metal band Dimmu Borgir plagiarised it almost note-by-note on their 1996 album ''Stormblåst''. I remember rolling on the floor from laughter when I borrowed the album from my girlfriend and first listened to it.

Apparently the Dimmu Borgir keyboard player passed off the composition as his own with the rest of the band, and when they were contacted by Tim Wright they denied everything.
