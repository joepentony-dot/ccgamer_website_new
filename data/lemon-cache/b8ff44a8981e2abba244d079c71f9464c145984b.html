hamakei 2024-01-26 (4/10)
I like the idea. It's nice having a game based on a movie which isn't just a side scrolling platform game. The concept of comic book style cut scenes with little mini games between could work really well.

The first 20 minutes or so are pretty good. Run away, drive across the map choosing where to go, come back from the dead....

And then you arrive in Midan and it all falls apart. Now you're button-mashing like one of those lame olympics-themed games, and wrangling with the joystick trying to climb a cliff-face, constantly falling down and trying over and over again.

Oh well. Never mind.

Colonel Kurtz 2023-06-28 (4/10)
This maddens me even more than the action game did, not for what it is, but for what it tries to be.

Almost every section or minigame is either silly or majorly unnerving. You could think of literally dozens of ways to make each minigame better, and without requiring a super-powered Amiga. The explorable locations are far too few to warrant a free-roam map (not that there were many more in the movie): it feels like it only exists for the annoyance of roadblocks! The manual says you can outrun the chasing monster by timing your button presses with arm positions but that's a lie. The platforming sections in Midian are every bit as excruciating as other commenters said. The Midian maze is confusing, with very few screens repeating over and over and not even a stupid compass. Versus fights are a walk in the park, while the "dodge the crosshair" minigame becomes crazy hard after the second time, and it's an instant kill too.

Another thing it shares with the NightBreed action game besides the license, is how it manages to be worse than Ocean's own Platoon from years ealier: the first-person underground section was much less contorted on that one and you could even get a hold of a map.

In their attempts to mock a Cinemaware title, all they managed to produce played no better than the Roger Rabbit tie-in and looked far worse - right, I wonder how anyone could like this shabby and talentless graphical style.

Kharn 2020-03-01 (4/10)
Just a 4 from me.

This game looks wonderful. There are so many well drawn scenes within this game, that it's almost worth playing for them alone. Unfortunately, that's where the praise ends.

Gameplay wise, it's essentially a bunch of mini-games. Not all that far removed from games you'd see on Saturday morning TV, where people would shout "Left, Right, Up Down!", over the phone. Or use the phone to directly control the action on-screen. Some involve choosing the correct path, so involve bashing buttons relentlessly, some involve navigating maze sections. None of which are really all that enjoyable and leave you wishing you'd played something else instead, like any Cinemaware or point and click title.

Spoiler: This may have been a passable title had it not been for the platofrming later in the game. It's absolutely atrocious, to the point where i don't think it was even tested all that much before release. It absolutely ruins what could have been a reasonable little adventure.

LeeIsAce 2009-09-26 (6/10)
I've just got back into Night Breed after almost 20 years away!

It's a mostly enjoyable game that captures the spooky spirit and atmosphere of the film (and the original Cabal novella) pretty well. There are quite a few memorable moments, like the graveyard chase (hammer the spacebar!) and the knife-thrower. Breaking out of the morgue is probably even scarier than intended, happening in one frame of animation that ALWAYS made me jump as a kid.

The big problem is that, upon reaching Midian, gameplay goes to shit. Boone is consigned to a slow crawl of pointing and clicking through a prohibitively confusing labyrinthe peppered with AWFUL fights against unfair enemies. If only a little more care was taken with design here, the difficulty dropped and Midian made a little more straightforward to navigate, the game would be great.
