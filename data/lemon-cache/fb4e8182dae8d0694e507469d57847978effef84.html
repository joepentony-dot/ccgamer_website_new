MartinJSUK 2023-01-12 (9/10)
This is possibly the most realistic Amiga game of any kind - there are millions of permutations for each shot, and all the spin, pace and contact adjustments worked exactly as they should have. The 3D was stunning by A500 standards as well, though admittedly such good results were achievable due to the motion being flat and not requiring real time input, and the trick shot mode was fun to play around with.

There were one or two niggles with it as a game - the difficulty curve was a little steep, and it's a shame you could only play individual frames rather than whole matches, and had to complete them to get the win credited even if you had an insurmountable lead - but it's still deservedly regarded as a classic.

I’ve got an ironic history with it - first got this as one of my few copies, from someone who only had an A1200 (on which it crashed), was impressed but found it hard, but sometime later I got the Award Winners Gold Edition compilation which had it, and armed with the manual I eventually got the hang of it, though I don't think I beat level 2. I played it so much the disk eventually wore out from saving the results and in-progress games to it, the only time that ever happened to me – if only I’d kept the copy. RIP Archer too.

I'm not surprised that this and Archer's Pool have about the same rating, but I'm surprised this has twice as many votes, given that snooker was such a UK game in those days, and pool was much more global.
