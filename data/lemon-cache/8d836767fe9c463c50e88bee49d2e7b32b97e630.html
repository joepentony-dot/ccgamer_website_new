James Bond 2004-03-14
For the people who were interested in trivia this game was not originally a James Bond game. I believe the original name was "Aquablast". I played this game in the late 1980s or early 1990s and it was pretty fun. The aiming was much better than the Living Daylights and the graphics were good for it's time. I remember that New Orleans was the only real mission there was. I think that after you beat New Orleans you got a message from M congratulating you on finished your mission and you get a chance to put your initials in the high score list. I seem to remember that when you got a certain number of points Mr. Big's heroin factory appeared and you could launch a missile at it by slowing down and hitting the FIRE button. Just in case people don't know how to fire the missile, slow down and hit the fire button. This game was pretty good for a bond licence. Quite an improvment over the more poor James Bond games such as "A View to a Kill".
compounded 2004-03-04
For all trivia freaks - Domark simply bought a game from Elite called Aquablasters (the unofficial follow up to Buggy Boy) and used it for their Bond license. Dave Thomas, the coder, must have liked programming 3D games as he also did First Strike.

WANDUS 2003-05-27
It was not a coincidence that 'Live and Let Die' was developed by Elite Systems, which had released 'Buggy Boy' about half a year before 'Life and Let Die'. Gameplay of the latter title is virtually identical to that of the former, the vehicles and the backdrops have been changed to fit the Bond-licence and now you get to blast your opponents. As far as I recall, hardly anyone bothered about the similarities, since ELITE had effectively improved an already very good game.
