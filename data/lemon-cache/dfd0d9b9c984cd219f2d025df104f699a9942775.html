nayweam 2025-12-10 (8/10)
I remember my first encounter with this game in 1988. My neighbour had a new Commodore 64 and he had this as a super turbo tape game. My neighbour, a pre-teen age-mate of mine, was mocking the game stupid and childish before it was even loaded!

To me, the game seemed intesresting, because it had many rooms I could go to. I was able to explore the play area freely, in what ever order I wanted to. The elevator animation was funny, the ghosts were scary. Since there were no instruction manual, we had to figure out what to do in the game. Diamonds needed to be put in the back of the car and TNT activated when shooting downwards, regardless of whether the big ghost was in the line of fire or not. If the police catched the robber, you could free yourself from prison to get a lost life back. Very clever!

I think the game is overall a pretty good, and for cheap game in 1985 I think it's even very good. The game has a very good playability and a strange hookability. My only complaint is the police, who could have been animated better. The police (and ghosts) also seem to always spawn at the worst possible moment, right in front of your face. I didn't like the helicopter either - I usually never like "flying" enemies in most games, who can go through walls, when I myself can't.

I understand that not everyone will be excited about such a simple and crude-looking game, but Zzap64's scoring feels a bit unfair.

Monk 2013-12-16
When we first played this game in the glorious 1980s with my friend, our first thought was: "Man, what a crummy-looking piece of crap".

Our second thought was: "But I'd be happy, if I could program a game like this.. "

After that, we really got into it, and realized that the seemingly crummy exterior really hides a solid game with balanced and exciting gameplay.

I just recently discovered the VIC-20-version (the original?), which has interesting differences. The sprites (I know the VIC-20 doesn't have sprites, but what am I going to call them? 'Characters' would be easy to misunderstand, although the 'sprites' in the VIC-20-version probably -are- made of characters, etc. ) in the VIC-20-version are much smaller, which means that the gameplay area is actually larger.

Monk 2013-12-16
With larger sprites, you have a smaller area (meaning, not as much 'maze' to walk around in).

The C64-version has more clarity with the "IN" sign instead of just a mystic 'arrow up'. The elevators also change places in the C64-version, and there are also little animations whenever something important happens (like going up on an elevator or being transported to jail), and the helicopter is new. The levels are completely different, although thematically similar.

With C64's sprite graphics, the sprites actually move smoothly, and the sprites resemble more clearly what they are supposed to be (which is nice on one hand, but on the other, destroys the immersion a bit - with a tiny character-police, that moves one char at the time, it's only natural for them to move the way they do - but with a identifiable police-sprite just sliding around without moving their arms or legs at all, looks a little weird and creepy).

The diamonds are huge in the VIC-20-version, whereas they look very tiny in the C64-version. They are probably relatively the same size factually, but because the main sprite size has changed, and the play area has changed accordingly, the diamonds appear to be just tiny specks, almost not even worth collecting.

Monk 2013-12-16
The policemen move a bit faster in the C64-version, I'd estimate, and the game is much harder because of it. In the VIC-20-version, I could easily get to the third floor, but with the C64-version, I sometimes got jailed by the first floor cops.

Both versions are great, although I can't even choose which would be better - they are both better than the other in different ways.

The main point is, this is a good game.

The only gripe is; what kind of morals and ethics it is teaching to people? First of all, diamonds are actually cheap, the prices are artificially inflated. Without women lusting for them so much, they would be sold very cheaply. Second of all, breaking the law should not be advocated, even in video games. I never understood why people cheer for the robbers and murderers in 'heist' movies and such. We really live in corrupt times, when even early video games advocated unlawful activities. Oh well, I guess stealing is better than murder (which is a solid part of the majority of video games, even back then).

This game is not good because of nostalgy; it's good, because it has a good gameplay.
