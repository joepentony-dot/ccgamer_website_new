ReTroViRuS 2011-04-26 (9/10)
This marks a temporary end-point in the evolution of the adventure game interface: The genre had begun with complex, text-based parsers that offered a wide variety of user inputs. Later, the point 'n click interface reduced the user's options to less then twenty verbs. Those verbs got fewer and fewer until they disappeared completely.

This bring us to Beneath a Steel Sky, which only offers two modes of interacting with the game world: Left mouse button to go to a location on the screen and right mouse button to use an object.

Maff Rignall 2007-03-14 (10/10)
Brilliant, brilliant. Not quite Monkey Island brilliant, but still a classic. And a good conversion of a PC game, with graphics and gameplay both surviving intact.

The graphics are among the best on the Amiga (and makes you wonder what Space Quest 4 would have been like in the hands of more capable converters). The Orwell-meets-Douglas-Adams storyline is dripping with black humour, and is a nice change from the self-referential gags of US graphic adventures. You have a great sidekick in the form of Joey, a great comic foil in the form of Lamb, and there's enough shifts and reveals in the story to keep you interested until the end... And it's a great ending too.

The only slight downside is the absence of things to do when you get stuck - the other characters won't talk to you unless it's plot-related, and will sometimes ignore you until you solve a particular puzzle. It's frustrating, but not enough to detract from the rest. Top stuff. 10/10.
