FincasKhalmoril 2025-09-02 (10/10)
While today the flaws of the game, especially the still too complicated handling of several units and the lack of formations, cannot be overlooked and dramatically lessen my joy, Dune 2 was at its time prophetic and visionary!

Let’s start: the game uses Frank Herbert‘s rich setting and puts it into a beautifully crafted game. While the Eye of the Beholder games showed what the Westwood Studies were able to do graphicwise, Dune 2 started to way a new generation of games had players dive into aatmospheric settings presented not like interactice movies but taking the best parts of movies and using them as interconnections between the games.
And these animated parts were stunning. The intro is even today quite well with regards to music use and pacing. The graphics are simply stunning. They do not copy David Lynch‘s style, but go their own way. And it’s a good way. There are speech samples, full blasts and the whole setting is wonderfully immersive.

Then the game itself: while later games (C&C specifically) worked on the handling with selecting units via a mousedrawn rectangle, Dune 2 had dynamic non-stop action, base building, harbesting, indirect support (ornithopters, fremen), npc-monsters (worms) and even nuclear blasts. In short the formula that was used for RTS up into the esrly 2000s was already fully developed. The only thing that was still a bit rough were the slighty imbalanced units and the too symmetric three sides.

For it’s time, this was a nearly perfect game!
