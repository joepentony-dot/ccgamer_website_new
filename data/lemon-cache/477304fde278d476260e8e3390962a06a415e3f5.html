Nayphee 2024-06-09 (7/10)
I recall playing this from cassette back in the day, and I remember it to be fun and quite challenging. It's semi-action, semi-puzzle as you control two goonies simultaneously to solve puzzles to get to the exit. You can play with one or two players. If playing solo, you press the fire button to switch between them.

It doesn't push the C64 to it's graphical and sonic limits though. In fact it looks like an Apple II or IBM port with those limited colours.
It's really the gameplay that matters here, and even when you've figured out how to complete each scene (the manual gives some clues), it's still pretty tricky to make it through to the end of all 8 screens.

Monk 2015-02-15
Pretty great, little game. Cool playability indeed, with either one player, or better yet, as a two-player game.

But why do all the Apple II-conversions look like Apple II-games, and all the Spectrum-conversions look like Spectrum-games?

Sure, it shows how versatile the Commodore 64 is (it can adapt to looking like all its competitors, except for Atari computers), but surely the games would have looked better, had they been enhanced to actually utilize the C64's capabilities and palette more fully.

Good musics, good playability, good animations, atmospheric backgrounds (especially the first screen in the house - starry sky and a house is always a winning combination, see Maniac Mansion for more of the similar, exciting atmosphere).

This is one of those rare cases, where the game is actually way better than the movie (though it's not saying much in this case, as PONG is better a game than goonies is a movie).
