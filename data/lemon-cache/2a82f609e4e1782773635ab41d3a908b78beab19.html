Sparky-D 2019-11-09 (10/10)
There are different versions of "Paradroid". The normal one from 1985, which is relative slow, compared to the other versions of the game, which came out later. 1986 the "Competition Edition" of the game was released which runs much faster at all. Sadly this version is not present here on Lemon64. 1989 then the "Metal Edition (Heavy Metal Paradroid)" followed, which had the faster speed from the Competition Edition together with different graphics. Can also be found here in Lemon64. Also not bad, but I always liked the original graphic of the game more. Then, around ten years ago, "Paradroid Redux" was released. It's an improved version of the game, done by "TNT". It's the superior version of all Paradroid's, which had a lot of advantages, for example it runs without slowdowns, even when a lot opponents are on the screen at the same time, has better sprite-collision and a lot more. In addition, at the beginning of the intro in the Redux version, you can also choose with the controller (up/down/left/right), which graphics (Normal/Metal Edition) you want in the game and whether you want the Competition or the Normal mode. In the Redux version you have basically everything in one place. Absolutely superb version and "Paradroid Redux" is my number-one game of all C64 games. And this means a lot, cause there are really a lot superb games on the C64. The rating I'm giving, is for the Redux version of the game, which unfortunately cannot be found separately, here in the archive.

Monk 2015-02-18 (10/10)
The Amiga version doesn't have multi-directional scrolling (how ironic that a "lesser" machine can do more!), and what scrolling it does have, is very jerky, thanks to the lazy Atari ST-conversion procedure instead of using the Amiga's strengths.

The original version has the best atmosphere, playability and graphics, a "bland" as some people think they are. Besides, just look at the robot images that you find from the consoles - they are anything but "bland"! As a kid, I was very excited to see those images, and they inspired me a great deal - and do so even today.

The Amiga version has these 'fully-drawn graphics', which are nicely done, and look pretty good, and I have no real complaints about them. But they make the atmosphere completely different. No longer is it this colorful, mystical spaceship with all kinds of imagined and unimaginable wonders going on, but this samey-colored, quite dull-looking place that unrealistically lets you see space through the windows (I mean, it's a multi-level ship, so how can you suddenly see through the layers right into space?).

WANDUS 2003-05-28
It became a classic because it effortlessly combined elements from various types of games (shoot 'em up, exploration / cartography, strategy, reaction, skill). Its storyline was additionally augmented by atmospheric 'database'-screens. This was quite a lot of variety for a 64kb-game. The challenge of liberating all space vessels was immensely captivating, due in large part to the great level of playability (a Braybrook speciality): success or failure depended entirely on the player's abilities. Lastly, it was a clever idea to make the main robot-sprites abstract, whilst presenting their actual looks only in the 'database'-files; whenever you met something like the dreaded '999', you shuddered as though you had remembered a horrible memento - your subconscious recollection of the detailed depiction of the droid in question! For the record, an 'update' called 'Heavy Metal Paradroid' was released two years later, featuring new levels as well as faster and smoother scrolling.
