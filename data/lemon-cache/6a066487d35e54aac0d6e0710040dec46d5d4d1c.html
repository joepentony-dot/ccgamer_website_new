Nayphee 2023-10-31 (7/10)
Very underrated fighting game. Uchi-Mata introduced special fighting moves from quarter-turns of the joystick before Street Fighter had the idea to use them. The end result is a fairly innovative fighting game involving a sport that doesn't get much attention in video games, but includes nice big player sprites, good animation, and unique gameplay.
You'll need the instructions to really make sense of the game and the rules of judo, but when it clicks for you, you're going to find it quite entertaining. You can also pull off illegal moves and get disqualified as well!

Thelead 2023-08-07 (9/10)
One of my favourite one on one fighters as it does something new that set it apart from the IK/Fist alikes at the time and could be looked on as a template for Street Fighter 2 in the way moves are pulled off. Large, well animated sprites, a CPU that does get better over a longer timeframe so you can make progress in single player. It shines as a 2 player game and is an essential 64 title.

Bailsofdoom 2015-01-17 (8/10)
I remember taking this game with me to my Nana's place from the "Set of the Best" tape compilation. It was the summer holidays of '94 and I'd recently obtained a massive swag of new second hand games and was testing some for the first time. I took out the instructions for Uchi Mata and dutifully set them in front of me, marveling at how similar some of the grappling moves were to thumb circle rolls from playing Street Fighter II. Only this came out in 1986! It took some time to master but achieving an uchi mata throw for the first time was extremely satisfying. When you consider the nature of the grappling art of Judo itself, wrestling with the inner and outer circles made perfect sense from a control point of view. A really creative and unusual martial arts game.
