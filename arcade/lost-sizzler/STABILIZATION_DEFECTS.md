## Architecture findings

- `v10-41-r59-live-regression-fixes.js` is the current final RAF owner. Before the stabilization clock candidate, visible active play called `update()` exactly once per accepted RAF with `dt=Math.min(45,gap)`, discarding active wall time above 45 ms per rendered frame under low FPS.
- The current Phase 3 candidate keeps R59 as the singleton RAF owner but gives only `dungeon-solo` bounded wall-time substeps: maximum 45 ms per substep, four substeps / 180 ms per rendered frame. It has no persistent debt accumulator, so pause/hidden time remains discarded at the accepted-RAF timestamp boundary. Horde, Spy and other controllers retain their established single-step path.
- The passive Solo diagnostics sample every 250 ms. A short pause entirely between two samples cannot be partitioned accurately by polling alone; if the second sample lands after resume, the whole interval can be counted as active. Lifecycle-stress windows therefore remain useful for ownership/depth evidence but must not be compared directly with uninterrupted wall-time cadence. The canonical soak now resets the diagnostics after lifecycle stress before taking the post-stress active timing sample.
- On branch candidate `a060447...`, uninterrupted accelerated soak baseline measured 1.002x, R59's independent pause cadence measured 30→30 (1.00 relative), and R60's Solo live integrity measured 1.02x→1.07x. These are the first current-candidate measurements showing the bounded clock near the intended 1.00x rate.
- `v10-41-mode-runtime.js` maintains mode lifecycle and owned-system gates from a separate 40 ms interval while also supplying the shared frame boundary.
- `v10-41-r30-owner-seal.js` checks normal movement ownership every 16 ms and can restore the golden `movePlayer` owner plus clear movement cooldowns.
- `v10-41-r30-global-movement-guard.js` maintains update/movement/damage ownership and movement-stall recovery on another 40 ms monitor. Current Phase 4 work now treats a callable damage function as unhealthy if required modern R56/R60 environmental owners are absent from ancestry; recovery reinstalls those required owners rather than accepting a bare historical function.
- R60's Solo cadence wrapper calls R30 `adoptReleaseMoveOwner()`. Once accepted, R30 stores that exact function as `goldenMove`; `v10-41-r30-owner-seal.js` then protects/reasserts that function through both a property assignment gate and a 16 ms seal. Current instrumented R60 Solo runs show this stack stable at four movement functions with one R60 cadence layer and no R30/seal repairs during the measured retry.
- `v10-41-r32-spy-loader.js` runs an always-on 20 ms monitor and calls `ensureR59()` globally; R59 then maintains its own 40 ms ownership interval.
- `v10-41-r57-desktop-prep-stability.js` retires R56's original periodic timer, then bridges retained R56 ownership, pending chest and combat work from its own 80 ms tick.
- Current damage ancestry can legitimately include a marker-neutral post-playtest wrapper above R60. Ownership validation therefore needs to reason about ancestry and semantics, not merely require R60 to be top-level.
- The fresh-start failure is now treated separately from Save & Quit → Continue: both can end with `runActive` absent, but one originates from a new Solo intent and the other from checkpoint restoration. Dedicated start-liveness instrumentation must identify the stalled owner before any broader recovery loop is added.
- Ownership/watchdog monitors are not being broadly removed yet. Consolidation proceeds only after the corrected bounded-clock soak proves stable and identifies residual live churn.

## Verification policy

A short CI smoke test is insufficient to close LS-SOLO-001 or LS-SOLO-003. Long-session timing defects require soak evidence. The target is sustained Solo simulation time approximately equal to active visible wall time, with pause/hidden time excluded and no later catch-up burst.
