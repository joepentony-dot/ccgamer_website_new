## Architecture findings

- `v10-41-r59-live-regression-fixes.js` is the current final RAF owner. Before the stabilization clock candidate, visible active play called `update()` exactly once per accepted RAF with `dt=Math.min(45,gap)`, discarding active wall time above 45 ms per rendered frame under low FPS.
- The current Phase 3 candidate keeps R59 as the singleton RAF owner but gives only `dungeon-solo` bounded wall-time substeps: maximum 45 ms per substep, up to 24 substeps / 1080 ms per accepted visible RAF. It has no persistent debt accumulator, so pause/hidden time remains discarded at the accepted-RAF timestamp boundary. Horde, Spy and other controllers retain their established single-step path.
- The passive Solo diagnostics sample every 250 ms. A short pause entirely between two samples cannot be partitioned accurately by polling alone; if the second sample lands after resume, the whole interval can be counted as active. Lifecycle-stress windows therefore remain useful for ownership/depth evidence but must not be compared directly with uninterrupted wall-time cadence. The canonical soak resets the diagnostics after lifecycle stress before taking the post-stress active timing sample.
- Earlier candidate measurements include an uninterrupted accelerated-soak baseline of 1.002x, R59 independent pause cadence of 30->30 (1.00 relative), and R60 Solo live integrity around 1.02x->1.07x. Exact head `71893eac3af...` was the first recorded all-seven-green canonical head after expansion to the 1080 ms / 24-step visible-gap ceiling and after adding focused damage-owner lifecycle coverage. Exact head `fc396757e376cf472b0b1e81d2c5268fa9688cd2` has now produced two full successful canonical Load Safety executions on the same code. Repeatability is improved, but long-session closure still requires the explicit sustained-play evidence in the defect exit criteria.
- `v10-41-mode-runtime.js` maintains mode lifecycle and owned-system gates from a separate 40 ms interval while also supplying the shared frame boundary.
- `v10-41-r59-live-regression-fixes.js` retains its 40 ms compatibility monitor, but its clock-owner maintenance is now drift-only. Healthy Solo measurement first reproduced repeated same-owner `R29.stableLoop` writes from `ensure()`; the current focused contract requires zero stable-loop writes/reassertions and zero Spy rule ticks during unchanged Solo.
- `v10-41-r29-loop-finalizer.js` retains notification rail/toast and lazy Spy loader maintenance, but its final-loop check now returns immediately when the live loop already has the stable R29/R59 marker. `v10-41-r30-global-movement-guard.js` also stops calling an already-cooperative R29 installer. The focused bridge-retirement browser contract keeps R30's movement watchdog monitor active while requiring zero stable-Solo R29 installer calls and zero R30 repairs.
- `v10-41-mode-runtime.js` now repairs a single requested owned-system gate from `ownedSystemState(name)` before returning diagnostics. This addresses the reproduced `updateTimed` race without waiting for the next broad lifecycle sweep and without adding another polling owner.
- `v10-41-r30-owner-seal.js` now prefers an assignment gate and the broader R30 global guard; its 16 ms fallback poll retires once either durable coverage path is active. This removes one continuous normal-mode polling owner from healthy Solo sessions while retaining a fallback for unsupported property-gate environments.
- `v10-41-r30-global-movement-guard.js` maintains update/movement/damage ownership and movement-stall recovery on another 40 ms monitor. Current Phase 4 work treats a callable damage function as unhealthy if required modern R56/R60 environmental owners are absent from ancestry; recovery reinstalls those required owners rather than accepting a bare historical function. Healthy Solo measurement shows this monitor is not mutating ownership in the quiet case, but it still performs repeated ancestry checks that should remain visible to Stage 7 review.
- R60's Solo cadence wrapper calls R30 `adoptReleaseMoveOwner()`. Once accepted, R30 stores that exact function as `goldenMove`; `v10-41-r30-owner-seal.js` then protects/reasserts that function through the assignment gate plus event-driven key checks, with its former 16 ms poll retired when equivalent coverage is present. Current instrumented R60 Solo runs show this stack stable with one R60 cadence layer and no R30/seal repairs during the measured retry.
- `v10-41-r32-spy-loader.js` no longer carries its former always-on 20 ms ordinary-Solo poll. Spy activation is driven by mode/lifecycle observation and actual Spy entry, and the focused R32 Solo diagnostic requires the retained polling timer to remain zero while ordinary Solo is active.
- `v10-41-r57-desktop-prep-stability.js` retires R56's original periodic timer and now narrows the retained R56 bridge to event/pending-driven synchronization. Its 80 ms timer still owns stall/contact/timed/Spy repairs, but stable Solo no longer re-runs the R56 owner bridge or combat maintenance while nothing changes.
- Current damage ancestry can legitimately include marker-neutral or Spy composition wrappers above R60. Ownership validation therefore reasons about complete ancestry and semantics, not merely whether R60 is the top-level function.
- The R60 damage property gate is optional in environments where the existing global `hurtPlayer` descriptor is non-configurable. Correctness cannot depend on that accessor; ancestry-aware composition and lifecycle restoration are the authoritative protection paths.
- The fresh-start failure is treated separately from Save & Quit → Continue: both can end with `runActive` absent, but one originates from a new Solo intent and the other from checkpoint restoration. Dedicated start-liveness instrumentation must identify the stalled owner before any broader recovery loop is added.
- The V10.25 melee/ammo layer installs `syncV125` around the then-current `sync`. Phase-by-phase canonical tracing established that `syncV125` may already be live before Spy starts, so the earlier Spy → Solo writer hypothesis is not retained. Quick Inventory correctness is now verified by synchronous canonical artwork ownership rather than by assuming a particular top-level `sync` identity.
- Named dossier presentation is an intentional gameplay modal: opening it with pause enabled changes `mode` from `playing` to `dossier` and clears input; closing it restores `playing`. Timing and movement fixtures must therefore exclude dossier wall time from active-play cadence measurements instead of treating the modal as a movement failure.
- Ownership/watchdog monitors are not being broadly removed. Consolidation proceeds one owner at a time after sustained timing evidence and targeted regression coverage identify a safe replacement boundary.

## Verification policy

A short CI smoke test is insufficient to close LS-SOLO-001 or LS-SOLO-003. Long-session timing defects require soak evidence. The target is sustained Solo simulation time approximately equal to active visible wall time, with pause/hidden time excluded and no later catch-up burst. A single all-green canonical head is supporting evidence, not closure; timing and ownership results must repeat before defects are closed.
